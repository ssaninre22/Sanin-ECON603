\renewcommand{\abstractname}{Abstract}
\begin{abstract} 
	\normalsize 
	\vspace{-5mm}
	
	\noindent Universities are major anchor institutions that can buffer local economies through stable payrolls and countercyclical enrollment, yet they may also create vulnerabilities when in-person activity contracts. This paper studies how university-dependent areas in the United States respond to national recessions from 1990 to 2024. Using county- and metropolitan-level unemployment data and a preliminary external classification of college towns, I find that university-oriented areas show slightly smaller unemployment increases in the 2001 and 2008 recessions, but these differences largely disappear during the COVID recession, when campus operations were disrupted. These patterns highlight a stability–fragility tradeoff in university-dependent economies. Ongoing work will construct endogenous measures of university exposure using QCEW and IPEDS and apply synthetic difference in differences and local projections to identify the dynamic role of universities in shaping regional economic resilience.	
	\\ 
	\vspace{-5mm}
	
	\onehalfspacing

\end{abstract}
